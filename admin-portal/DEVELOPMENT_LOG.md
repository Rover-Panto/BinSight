# Development and model-correction log

This log records material decisions made before the packaged result. Exploratory results are not final evidence.

1. **Scenario localization.** The earlier configurable Malaysian/Johor template was replaced with Subang Jaya. Residential generation changed to the MBSJ 1.90 kg/person/day indicator and DOSM 3.7-person household size. The depot moved to the provisional Batu Tiga/Subang Jaya waste-transfer feature.

2. **Dutch equipment archetype.** Bin volume changed to the VDL 4.5 m3 UGC form. The vehicle became a Maxxum/UGS-family collection archetype with a 22 m3 body, 1,500 kg lift reference, and conservative configurable 9,000 kg payload.

3. **Three-bin controller topology.** The physical design was locked to exactly three bins per microprocessor. The district generator, configuration checks, sensor schema, firmware, and gateway now enforce this invariant.

4. **Capacity sizing.** At 3,603.6 kg/day, 540 kg/bin, 80% design fill, a three-day interval, and 1.25 reserve, the district requires 10.43 three-bin sites. This was rounded up to 11 sites and 33 bins.

5. **OSM backend change.** Multiple public Overpass endpoints timed out or rate-limited during the build. The road backend was changed to OSRM services over OSM. The table response, requested/snapped coordinates, hash, and route geometries are cached. This is still OSM-road routing, but OSRM table distance is fastest-route road distance rather than a locally computed shortest-edge path.

6. **Initial 11-site allocation.** Preliminary anchors were placed across SS12-SS19, USJ 1/2/4, and Bandar Sunway. All anchors snapped to a drive service within 22.1 m.

7. **Exploratory policy study.** Small four-replication development blocks compared 48-, 60-, and 72-hour batching and route budgets. Longer gaps reduced distance but caused unacceptable overflow. A 48-hour minimum gap, 65% current / 105% upper-predicted dispatch trigger, 54% / 104% inclusion thresholds, and 30 km optional-stop budget were locked before production evaluation.

8. **Local capacity correction.** A first district-level evaluation revealed that the total 33-bin capacity was sufficient but five individual sites slightly exceeded their own reserved three-bin capacity. Those results were discarded. Household allocation was rebalanced while preserving all 11 locations, 500 households, 20 commercial units, and 3,603.6 kg/day. A new code guard now rejects any locally overloaded site.

9. **Routing timeout correction.** The first run after rebalancing stopped because OR-Tools returned no solution within the 250 ms limit for one feasible dispatch. The model now falls back to a deterministic capacity-packed nearest-neighbour route and records `routing_fallbacks`. A dedicated unit test verifies capacity and complete service. The final run used this fallback zero times.

10. **First validation isolation.** The original 30-replication result used arrival seeds beginning at base +710,000 and sensor seeds at base +720,000, separate from the +510,000/+520,000 exploratory blocks and discarded +610,000/+620,000 structurally invalid run. The failed timeout attempt did not produce or inspect policy results; it was rerun unchanged after the general solver-reliability fix.

11. **First holdout finding.** The original smart policy improved stop selectivity but worsened overflow, trips, distance, fuel, CO2, and truck utilization. That result was retained as a transparent failure finding and motivated a user-requested safety/fuel redesign rather than being presented as successful.

12. **Safety/fuel redesign.** The controller now converts the conservative 48-hour growth forecast into `time_to_overflow_hours` and `risk_level`. Critical bins can override the normal 48-hour dispatch gap. Useful sibling bins at the same three-bin site are collected without added road travel, while other optional bins are admitted only when their incremental proxy distance is within a configurable limit. No bins or sites were added.

13. **Second exploratory block.** The original +710,000/+720,000 seed block became development data for the redesign. Small paired trials selected a 20-hour emergency horizon and 5 km maximum optional incremental distance, with overflow treated as a hard acceptance constraint. Because those seeds had been inspected, they were not reused for the new packaged result.

14. **Startup-artifact discovery; prior result withdrawn.** The replacement holdout initially appeared to show 5.08% lower distance/fuel. Audit showed that the fixed policy swept all 33 bins at hour 6 on day zero even though every bin began empty. That one artificial 39.56 km sweep was larger than the reported total advantage. Removing it reversed the distance conclusion, so the packaged claim was withdrawn and the baseline was corrected before further reporting.

15. **Observation and leakage correction.** Hidden physical mass is now private simulation state. A separate seeded sensor model adds noise, bias, drift, outliers, missing readings, disagreement, confidence, and uncertainty. Forecast/dispatch features contain observations and history only; hidden fill is used only for labels and outcome measurement. Input validation now handles stale/future timestamps and preserves an aged last-valid reading instead of converting uncertainty to zero.

16. **Chronological operations correction.** The SimPy clock changed to minutes. OSRM durations, traffic bands, eight-minute service, depot unloading, inter-trip turnaround, and sequential trips now consume time. Waste continues to arrive during the trip, and a bin is emptied only when its service completes. The two-trip limit applies to the entire calendar day rather than independently at 06:00 and 18:00.

17. **Fuel-model correction.** Fuel is now decomposed into base driving, traffic, payload, collection idle, and depot idle components. CO2 follows total fuel. These remain configurable assumptions and are not a measured truck model.

18. **Map and replay correction.** Artificial offsets for three bins at a site were removed. All route maps now use 11 consolidated site markers with three-bin popups, attention counts, bounded Subang Jaya navigation, and accessible states. Mock tracking interpolates along saved road geometry and respects travel/service/depot timestamps.

19. **Stress evaluation added.** Base, 1.45× high-demand, 1.35× traffic, 18% missing/8% outlier sensor-failure, and 0.65× truck-capacity conditions use 30 paired replications each. Raw and equal three-day post-warm-up metrics are reported separately; scenarios are not pooled.

20. **Integer-capacity boundary correction.** During the definitive stress run, a rare load passed floating-point preselection but exceeded OR-Tools capacity after each demand was rounded upward. Preselection, exact fallback packing, proxy routing, and OR-Tools now share the same conservative integer demand rule. A regression test fixes the boundary behavior.

21. **Policy tuning lock.** Separate development seeds at base +1,010,000/+1,020,000 compared 48-, 60-, and 72-hour dispatch gaps and tighter optional/sibling rules under base and high demand. The 48-hour gap and current sibling/5 km rule retained the best safety-first fuel trade-off. Shorter emergency horizons reduced fuel but allowed materially more overflow, so the 20-hour emergency horizon was retained.

22. **Definitive validation seed.** Because earlier smoke/audit runs exposed the +910,000/+920,000 seeds, they are not called a holdout. The definitive result uses untouched arrival seeds beginning at base +1,310,000 and sensor seeds at base +1,320,000 across all five declared scenarios. Exact results are recorded in `FINAL_RESULTS.md`; any earlier 5.08% saving statement is obsolete.

23. **Routing AAR and objective correction.** Review of the locked result found that threshold-first dispatch and distance-only optional batching did not directly optimize the stated outcomes: avoid wasteful trips, reduce distance and prevent overflow. Dynamic policy v2 uses one shared validation/planning path for simulation, CLI and portal, and makes waiting an explicit alternative to paying fixed-trip, road, time, service and low-fill costs. Emergency/service-level stops remain mandatory.

24. **Producer contract and identity correction.** Simulation service groups were separated from the physical Teensy 4.1/C3 pilot profile. Telemetry-routing schema 2.0 now preserves event/boot identity, per-bin acquisition and receipt time, clock state, source mode, channel availability/quality and explicit unknown forecast states. The legacy ESP32 reference was moved to schema 1.1 with boot-aware deduplication so a controller restart does not discard a valid sequence reset.

25. **Forecast evaluation correction.** Timestamp-windowed features, collection resets and observation gaps replace row-adjacent assumptions. A full 48-hour purge separates train, calibration and untouched holdout windows. A q90 adjustment is fit on calibration only; holdout coverage, coverage error, pinball loss and interval width are recorded. A missing-channel tuple fallback found by the full experiment received a regression test.

26. **Durable planning lifecycle.** A schema-versioned SQLite store now retains immutable snapshots, source event references, plan versions and lifecycle transitions. A single-owner opt-in runner creates drafts only; repeated input is idempotent, mock dispatch is one-per-accepted-plan, and active-trip replanning freezes the current leg and creates a linked suffix draft rather than mutating history.

27. **Changed-policy evidence isolation.** Dynamic v2 results are written to `artifacts/dynamic_v2/` so rerunning the changed policy cannot overwrite historical v1 evidence. The simulation calls the public validator and route builder every six hours, eliminating a separate model-only threshold implementation.

28. **Trip-value probability correction before v2 lock.** The first full development run assigned every medium 48–72 hour risk band a flat 5% probability of overflow in the next 12 hours. Eleven bins around 25–35% fill could therefore aggregate enough nominal benefit to justify a 37 km route even though none was mandatory. Base post-warm-up distance rose to 1,729.86 km versus 511.73 km fixed, so that run was rejected and kept out of the packaged result. The proxy now decays with `(12 / time_to_overflow)^2` beyond the next opportunity, while q90 crossings inside 12 hours remain service constraints. A regression test fixes the 69-hour aggregation failure.

29. **Planning-opportunity alignment.** A four-replication screen after the probability correction still produced 754.88 km base post-warm-up distance versus 511.66 km fixed because 25.5 dynamic trips were compared with 18 fixed trips. The simulator reevaluates at six-hour observations while the service gate assumed the next chance was 12 hours away. That candidate was rejected; subsequent work uses the actual six-hour opportunity and keeps uncertain evidence below the 90% emergency boundary as inspection unless scheduled same-site consolidation is independently justified.

30. **Physical architecture correction.** The latest owner handoff establishes one general-waste and two recycling-return fill channels on the shared Teensy/PR #2 relay. Telemetry-routing 2.1 preserves `event_kind`, `bin_type` and registry-owned `waste_stream`; all three fill channels can route, recognition/session events from PR #3 cannot, and one truck trip cannot mix incompatible streams.

31. **Service-memory correction.** Completion now writes durable per-bin service events. A delayed pre-service reading is superseded by a zero post-service state until genuinely newer acquisition evidence arrives, preventing a collected bin from being scheduled twice. The UI exposes completion only after accepted mock dispatch.

32. **Patterned demand revision.** Demand now multiplies normalized hourly, seven-day, cyclic monthly and continuous annual factors with targeted buildup/peak/decay events, bounded trend/change points, district/local lognormal AR(1) regimes and non-negative Gamma arrivals. Two years of synthetic history are separate from the 30-day operational evaluation. Eleven scenarios cover normal, seasonal, event, persistent/local surge, trend, change, traffic, sensing, capacity and combined stress.

33. **Forecast revision.** Observable acquisition-time features include 6/24/168-hour growth, month/year cycles, time since service, historical bin behavior and only events known at prediction time. Purged train/calibration/holdout windows use the complete 168-hour maximum horizon. Models report 6/24/48/168-hour MAE, q90 quality, time-to-overflow error, alert precision/recall, Brier scores and calibration bins. Hidden unannounced-event intensity is never a feature before `known_at`.

34. **Dynamic-v2 policy lock.** Development screens at +1,310,000/+1,320,000 rejected one-stop six-hour alarms, unconstrained 48-hour consolidation and low-fill uncertainty padding. The locked candidate anchors optional batches every 72 hours from the common empty start, preserves immediate service constraints, requires 45% central fill unless forecast service is due before the next batch, and allows high uncertain evidence to join—but not create—an independently justified scheduled site. The last screen still showed a normal-demand distance trade-off, so tuning stopped rather than forcing a smart-policy win.

35. **Untouched v2 evaluation.** The definitive 30-pair, eleven-scenario run uses arrival seeds beginning at base +1,610,000 and sensor seeds beginning at base +1,620,000. Its policy, configuration and scenario set were locked before those results were viewed; exact evidence is isolated under `artifacts/dynamic_v2/`.

36. **PR #2 forecast bridge.** The current PR #2 history API is now consumed through an explicit, read-only adapter rather than treating its current-fill overflow badge as prediction. The adapter preserves acquisition/ingestion time, rejects future/conflicting evidence, separates collection resets from generation, prevents isolated ultrasonic jumps from alarming, learns gated calendar/event patterns with site/area fallbacks, updates recent rates/residuals online, and emits complete probabilistic PR #1 snapshots with null uncalibrated weight. A routing-owned append-only cache works around the API's 2,000-row response cap without modifying producer data. Deterministic rolling-origin acceptance evidence covers all requested regimes and exposes weak seven-day/drift coverage; physical performance remains blocked on retained three-channel PR #2 history.

37. **Four-material demonstration topology.** The competition simulation now has one general-waste, plastic, metal, and glass bin at each of 11 sites (44 bins total). Material-specific density and mass fractions replace the former three-bin simulation assumption. The existing three-channel physical profile remains explicit and incomplete rather than silently pretending that a fourth sensor exists.

38. **Destination-aware recycling routes.** Plastic, metal, and glass share a dry-recycling stream that must unload at the provisional MBSJ USJ 9 Recycling Centre before returning to the depot. General waste unloads at the waste depot. Separate return matrices make that destination part of optimization cost, dispatch payloads, simulation timing evidence, and map geometry.

39. **Historical replay and service-memory review fixes.** Forecast states trained after a simulated decision time are rejected and rebuilt from then-available observations. A completed-service empty override now expires after a bounded grace period unless a newer post-service sensor reading arrives, preventing both future-model leakage and indefinitely frozen empty bins.

40. **Local demonstration hardening.** Startup now fails closed on configuration, matrix, network, SQLite, and storage readiness; logs rotate locally; state has a consistent backup command; Streamlit binds only to loopback; remote HTTP telemetry is rejected; dispatch remains mock-only; and public keyless map tiles have a light fallback.

41. **Four-bin smoke finding.** A bounded two-pair normal-scenario run verified end-to-end execution without routing fallbacks or unfinished trips. In those seeds, dynamic routing reduced mean overflow incidents from 6 to 4 and wasted pickups from 197.5 to 39.5, but increased mean distance from 766.0 km to 1,273.6 km and trips from 18 to 36. This is an integration result, not statistical or production evidence; the distance objective is not yet met.

42. **Distance-number tuning rejected.** Matched development screens varied conservative emergency fill from 90–96%, optional batching gaps/costs, sibling inclusion, and two-/three-hour replanning. Simple threshold increases saved distance by waiting longer but worsened overflow; broader consolidation delayed urgent service. The untouched four-pair finalist used three-hour replanning and a 93% conservative emergency threshold. It lowered normal-demand distance 5.3% and overflow incidents, but raised high-demand distance 5.0%, added four high-demand trips on average, and left unfinished horizon trips. Because no static candidate met distance and safety guardrails across both declared conditions, `config.json` was left unchanged and the next proposed improvement is regime-aware routing logic rather than coefficient hunting.

43. **Structural distance follow-up rejected.** New disjoint development and confirmation seeds tested optional route-admission batches, marginal-detour caps, surge fallbacks, 12–48 hour deadline admission, longer OR-Tools budgets and safety-constrained asymmetric 2-opt. Admission and the 12-hour deadline traded distance for overflow; 18–48 hours reproduced the existing policy; longer search found only metre-scale improvements. Protected 2-opt preserved stops and mandatory arrival bounds locally but changed later decision timing: untouched normal distance rose 2.5% and high-demand distance rose 0.5%, with worse overflow exposure. The feature remains explicitly disabled. The next candidate is recycling-facility-to-next-trip deadhead chaining, contingent on trusted facility-origin matrices and operator approval to skip an intermediate depot return.

44. **Specialized two-truck and deadline-routing revision.** The active design now has exactly one general-waste truck at the waste depot and one three-compartment recycling truck at the USJ 9 facility. Recycling routes begin/end at that facility; general routes never visit it; either truck can dispatch while the other is active, but no extra truck is created. A three-day rolling assignment schedules forecast-due work, active suffix replanning is stream- and residual-capacity-safe, and same-day routing constrains modeled arrival—not just departure—against time to overflow. The built-in demo gives UGB-001 and UGB-005 the same 6.3-hour deadline to show why one truck leaves before the next six-hour observation. Live tracking now selects either specialized vehicle from a fresh paired 30-day artifact and renders each four-bin site's maximum forecast fill as a grey-to-red gauge. Two paired replications across eleven scenarios passed integration, but distance/trips remain higher than fixed in the reported seeds, so no production-performance claim is made.

These are engineering-development corrections, not confirmatory hypothesis testing. `FINAL_RESULTS.md` is the historical v1 lock; `DYNAMIC_V2_RESULTS.md` is the historical versioned v2 comparison. Neither matches the active four-bin configuration or represents measured field performance.
